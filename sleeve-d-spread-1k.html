The experiment

What this instance tests, how, and what would settle it.
About this experimentSleeve A's favorite-bid rule restricted to books where the quoted spread exceeds 2¢ — the one conditioning variable (of 8 scanned) that improved the sleeve out-of-sample (+3.21% vs +2.40%). The open question it exists to answer: do wide-spread books actually fill a resting bid, or is the extra edge exactly where fills never happen? ($1k twin: sizes the tape can prove.)342 quotes · 94 fills · 56 settled so far▼

Why this instance exists

Research sweep 2026-08-24, family 'conditioning'. Validation: +3.21% [+1.54,+4.79] vs unconditioned +2.40%; the improvement itself is +0.81pt with CI [-0.07,+1.71] (P(diff<=0)=3.3%) — marginal. At mid execution the conditional edge nearly vanishes (+0.38%), i.e. it is mostly spread capture concentrated in thin books where the fill assumption is weakest. This instance is the fill-reality test of that exact concern. $1k realistic-size twin (2026-08-25): identical strategy to its $10k parent, bankroll 1000 — position caps drop ~10x ($50/market), so credited sizes sit inside what the public tape can actually verify. Purpose: (1) the tape-verified floor should track the headline P&L closely here, unlike at $10k; (2) any performance divergence between the twins isolates size-driven artifacts in the fill model. Fill events, win rates, and per-contract ROI are size-independent by construction, so the twins should agree on those.

Hypothesis

Kalshi favorites priced 80¢–95¢ are almost perfectly calibrated near resolution (12-month backtest: 7,188 snapshots from 2,712 settled markets). A maker who joins the best bid instead of paying the ask keeps the spread and pays only the 2%×p(1−p) maker fee, which the backtest says is worth about +2.49% per contract — if fills were free.

They are not. A resting bid is filled disproportionately when the market is moving against it (the real makers pull their quotes; the dumb one gets hit). That adverse-selection haircut is the number this instance measures, with zero money at risk.

What happens each cycle

Every 5 minutes: scan open markets in Politics, Elections, World, Economics, Companies, Science and Technology closing in 2 h – 3 days (combo shards excluded), keep those whose favorite mid is in 80¢–95¢, spread ≤ 10¢, 24 h volume ≥ 25; quote up to 10 new markets per cycle, at most 40 resting.

A quote is a pretend bid on the favorite side at its best bid — joining the queue, with the contracts already ahead of us recorded. One market is never quoted twice.

How a fill is decided

No order exists on the exchange, so a fill is inferred from public data, two ways:

Tape: trades printing on our side at our price (beyond the contracts that were queued ahead of us) or through it — i.e. real sellers hit the level we were resting at.

Book-through: the best ask on our side drops to or below our bid — a crossing order would have executed against us.

Queue priority is approximated from top-of-book sizes, and a fill is assumed to take our whole size. Both are optimistic; they make the measured haircut a lower bound, not an upper one.

Follow-up and exits

Follow the bid up, never chase: the bid is moved up with the market only while price + fee ≤ favorite mid at placement (this instance: ceiling ON). Above the ceiling the order holds where it is.

Pulled if the favorite's mid collapses below 78¢, after 48 h unfilled, or within 1 h of close. Pulled quotes are still followed to resolution: they form the quoted-but-unfilled baseline.

Position size

Paper bankroll $1,000. Each quote is sized with 0.25× Kelly: f* = (q − c) / (1 − c) with c = price + fee and q = the favorite's mid when our price was set (the calibrated-market assumption), capped at 5% of bankroll per market, 10% per correlated event and 25% of the market's 24 h volume (a resting order cannot be filled for multiples of what trades in a day), floor 1 contract; re-sized when the price moves.

Sizing only scales the dollar figures; the measurement itself is per contract and unaffected by it.

What is measured

Fill rate and time-to-fill — do resting bids get hit, and how fast.

Realized ROI per contract on settled fills: (payout − cost) / cost, payout 1 or 0, cost = price + fee. Haircut = backtest benchmark +2.49% − realized ROI.

Selection gap: win rate of the favorites we were filled on vs the favorites we quoted but were not filled on. If fills happen mostly when things go wrong, the filled win rate will sit below the unfilled one — that gap is adverse selection made visible.

EV at fill (q − cost, q = mid when priced) and EV now (today's mid − cost) on every open fill; dollars are contracts × per-contract.

What would confirm or kill it

Run 2–4 weeks, target 300+ settled fills. Ignore the ROI sign in week one — single 90¢ losers swing it wildly early on.

Survives if realized maker ROI stays positive after the haircut and fill rates make the capital math work. Dies if the haircut eats the +2.49%, or if fills are so rare the bankroll idles. Going live would additionally need both owners' sign-off; nothing in this repository places real orders.

Assumptions and caveats

Simulated fills on real quotes — no queue priority data, full-size fills assumed, and a real resting order would itself change the book. The five instances quote overlapping markets, so their results are correlated, not independent samples. Kalshi public API only, polled politely; no account, no keys.

How to read this page

Insights carries the headline numbers and the funnel. Resting orders are live pretend bids (click for the rule, the queue and the size). Fills are bids the tape says would have been hit, with their current mark. Settled outcomes are every quote, filled or not, at resolution. Titles link to the market on Kalshi. The pill at the top turns red if no cycle has landed in 15 minutes.

The experiment

What this instance tests, how, and what would settle it.
About this experimentLogs both venues' books for reviewed pairs of equivalent markets every 5 minutes: the mid gap, and what a maker resting at the bids on both venues would lock after fees (Kalshi 1.75%, Polymarket 0%). Measures whether cross-venue disagreement is frequent and persistent enough to matter — and which venue is the better place to make.11 pairs · 17467 observations · cross edge > 0 69.2%▼

Why this instance exists

Backlog item 2, measurement half. It does not trade; it asks whether the two venues disagree enough to matter and which is the better place to make markets (Polymarket charges makers nothing). Pairings are the fragile part — every one carries a logged rules-reading, and resolution agreement is the audit.

Hypothesis

Kalshi and Polymarket list the same questions under different rules, fees and crowds. If their prices disagree often and for long enough, a maker quoting both venues — a YES bid on the cheap one, a NO bid on the dear one — could lock a spread that neither venue offers on its own, with Polymarket's 0% maker fee on one leg. The brief calls this the venue counterfactual for sleeve A.

The nuance is that resting at the bid on both venues always locks the combined spread minus fees — that is ordinary market making. The cross-venue signal is only the part that beats the better single venue's two-sided quote.

How pairs are chosen

Markets are matched by hand after reading both rule texts: the candidates command lists crude title matches (currently over Kalshi markets in Politics, Elections, World, Economics, Companies, Science and Technology closing within 14 days), and a pair enters data/venue_pairs.json only with a logged rules-reading and a confidence. Near-misses are logged as rejected — e.g. Kalshi's Trump–Putin market counts phone calls while Polymarket's needs an in-person meeting, which is why they trade 30+ cents apart.

Inverted pairs are ones where Polymarket's YES is Kalshi's NO (e.g. a 'fewer than 25' bucket against an 'above 25' strike) and are flipped before comparison. At resolution the logger records whether the two venues actually agreed — the empirical test of every pairing.

What happens each cycle

Every 5 minutes, for each tracked pair: the Kalshi market (batched) and both Polymarket CLOB books (YES token and NO token). One observation is logged when both venues are two-sided; one-sided books are logged but not scored.

Fees assumed: Kalshi maker 1.75% / taker 7%, Polymarket maker 0% / taker 4%, each × p(1−p). Nothing is ever sent to either venue.

What is measured

Raw maker lock = 1 − (YES bid + fee) on one venue − (NO bid + fee) on the other, best of the two orientations. Positive whenever the two spreads together exceed fees, so it is nearly always positive and is shown only as context.

Cross-venue edge = maker lock − max(single-venue two-sided lock on Kalshi, on Polymarket). This is the headline: it is positive only when the venues' prices disagree by more than the tighter venue's spread.

Taker arb = crossing both spreads at the asks after taker fees — the naive 'arbitrage', almost never positive.

Mid gap (Kalshi mid − Polymarket mid) and each venue's best spread — which venue is the more competitive place to make in.

What would confirm or kill it

Run alongside the paper traders for the same 2–4 weeks. Interesting if the cross-venue edge is positive a meaningful share of the time (say a fifth of observations or more) on liquid pairs and persists across consecutive cycles — long enough for two resting orders to fill. Dead if it is rare, fleeting, or confined to wide-spread illiquid pairs, in which case the spread comparison alone answers the venue question.

Any pair where the venues disagree at resolution was a bad pairing: its history is discarded and the rules-reading corrected.

Assumptions and caveats

A 'lock' assumes both resting bids fill — the very assumption the paper traders are haircutting. Polymarket's taker fee tier varies by category (politics assumed). Top-of-book only; no queue or depth modelling. Both APIs are public and polled politely.

How to read this page

Insights has the headline rates. Pairs lists each pairing with its rules-reading, both venues' live quotes, the lock, the edge and its history; both titles link out. Recent cross-venue edges is the tape of moments the cross-venue quote beat the better single venue.

Across 17467 observations on 9 pairs, quoting across the two venues beat the better single-venue two-sided quote 69.2% of the time (mean edge when it did +1.35¢, best +22.00¢) — that is the cross-venue signal.

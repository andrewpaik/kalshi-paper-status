The experiment

What this instance tests, how, and what would settle it.
About this experimentRests a simulated YES bid and a NO bid at the best bids of wide-spread markets (4–15¢, mids 15–85¢) in the same categories, and measures how often both legs fill (locking the spread) versus one leg (a directional leftover held to settlement).2226 quotes · 1572 fills · 1498 settled so far▼

Why this instance exists

A different bet from Sleeve A: market making rather than a directional favorite. It does not care which side is the favorite; it wants wide spreads and a completed round trip. Its failure mode is the same adverse selection, showing up as one-legged leftovers.

Hypothesis

On markets with a wide spread (≥ 4¢) a maker who rests both a YES bid and a NO bid earns the spread whenever both legs fill: owning YES at a and NO at b pays exactly 1 at settlement, locking 1 − a − b − fees regardless of the outcome. When only one leg fills the maker is left with a directional position — and adverse selection says the leg that fills is the one the market moved against.

This instance measures the round-trip rate (how often both legs fill), the locked spread when they do, and what the one-legged leftovers cost at settlement.

What happens each cycle

Every 5 minutes: scan open markets in Politics, Elections, World, Economics, Companies, Science and Technology closing in 2 h – 3 days, keep those with a YES spread between 4¢ and 15¢ (wide enough to pay, narrow enough that the mid means something), YES mid in 15¢–85¢ (off the tails) and 24 h volume ≥ 25; quote up to 10 new markets per cycle, at most 40 legs resting.

A quote is a pair: a pretend YES bid at the YES best bid and a pretend NO bid at the NO best bid, each joining its own queue.

How a fill is decided

No order exists on the exchange, so a fill is inferred from public data, two ways:

Tape: trades printing on our side at our price (beyond the contracts that were queued ahead of us) or through it — i.e. real sellers hit the level we were resting at.

Book-through: the best ask on our side drops to or below our bid — a crossing order would have executed against us.

Queue priority is approximated from top-of-book sizes, and a fill is assumed to take our whole size. Both are optimistic; they make the measured haircut a lower bound, not an upper one.

Follow-up and exits

Each leg is managed on its own: follow its side's best bid up only while price + fee ≤ that side's mid at placement; pulled after 48 h unfilled or within 1 h of close. There is no favorite-collapse rule here (a two-sided quote has no favorite). A leg that fills is held to settlement whether or not its partner fills; pulled legs are followed to resolution as the unfilled baseline.

Position size

Paper bankroll $10,000. Each quote is sized with 0.25× Kelly: f* = (q − c) / (1 − c) with c = price + fee and q = the favorite's mid when our price was set (the calibrated-market assumption), capped at 5% of bankroll per market, 10% per correlated event and 25% of the market's 24 h volume (a resting order cannot be filled for multiples of what trades in a day), floor 1 contract; re-sized when the price moves. Both legs of a pair get the same size.

Sizing only scales the dollar figures; the measurement itself is per contract and unaffected by it.

What is measured

Fill rate and time-to-fill — do resting bids get hit, and how fast.

Realized ROI per contract on settled fills: (payout − cost) / cost, payout 1 or 0, cost = price + fee. Haircut = backtest benchmark +2.49% − realized ROI.

Selection gap: win rate of the favorites we were filled on vs the favorites we quoted but were not filled on. If fills happen mostly when things go wrong, the filled win rate will sit below the unfilled one — that gap is adverse selection made visible.

EV at fill (q − cost, q = mid when priced) and EV now (today's mid − cost) on every open fill; dollars are contracts × per-contract.

Pairs: both legs filled (locked 1 − cost_yes − cost_no) vs one leg; realized P&L sums both legs, so a locked pair shows up as one small win and one loss that net to the lock.

What would confirm or kill it

Run 2–4 weeks, target 300+ settled fills. Ignore the ROI sign in week one — single 90¢ losers swing it wildly early on.

Survives if realized maker ROI stays positive after the haircut and fill rates make the capital math work. Dies if the haircut eats the +2.49%, or if fills are so rare the bankroll idles. Going live would additionally need both owners' sign-off; nothing in this repository places real orders.

Assumptions and caveats

Simulated fills on real quotes — no queue priority data, full-size fills assumed, and a real resting order would itself change the book. The five instances quote overlapping markets, so their results are correlated, not independent samples. Kalshi public API only, polled politely; no account, no keys.

How to read this page

Insights carries the headline numbers and the funnel. Resting orders are live pretend bids (click for the rule, the queue and the size). Fills are bids the tape says would have been hit, with their current mark. Settled outcomes are every quote, filled or not, at resolution. Titles link to the market on Kalshi. The pill at the top turns red if no cycle has landed in 15 minutes.
